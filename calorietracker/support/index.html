Common questions

Do I need an API key?

No. The app estimates on its own keys, under a daily limit shared between everyone using it. Adding your own key at the bottom of Settings is an override: that provider then talks to your account directly, nothing passes through the app's server, and the shared limit stops applying to it.

A model is labelled "needs your own key"

That provider isn't funded on the shared keys, so it's only available if you supply a key for it. Pick a different model, or add a key for that provider in Settings. Keys are per provider and held in the iOS Keychain.

I've hit the daily limit

The shared allowance resets daily. If you'd rather not wait or you use the app heavily, add your own API key for one provider — that removes the shared limit for it. Keys come from Google AI Studio, Anthropic or OpenAI.

The calorie estimate looks wrong

It's an estimate from a photo, and it can be. You can edit it before saving — the model proposes, you decide. After saving, tap the entry on Today or in History to adjust the portion weight; the calories and macros rescale with it. You can also refile it under a different meal or delete it there.

It asked me a question about my meal

That's deliberate, and only happens when the leading model says it isn't sure. The app asks the one question that would most improve the estimate — "was the chicken grilled or fried?" — and re-runs it on your fallback model with your answer. Confident estimates never trigger it. Set the fallback to None in Settings if you'd rather just see the unsure estimate and correct it yourself.

An entry is stuck waiting to be estimated

Nothing is lost. If you were offline, or the provider was busy, the entry is saved on your phone with its photos and estimated in the background. Offline entries go the moment you're back online. A busy model is retried after one minute, then twice more, and if it's still busy the entry switches to your backup model on its own. Today shows what each queued entry is waiting for and when the next attempt lands, with a Retry button if you'd rather not wait.

My entry was filed under the wrong meal

Meals are assigned from the photo's capture time, so an entry is filed under when you ate, not when you got around to logging it. Typed entries with no photo use the current time. Tap the entry to refile it.

There's no Workout tab

You said no to training during setup, which removes the tab entirely. Turn it on in Settings and it comes back, along with the questions about your equipment and sport.

I changed my equipment, sport or body stats — did I lose anything?

No. Changing any of it in Settings keeps your place in the session rotation and everything you've logged.

The app isn't suggesting more weight

Progression stays quiet until it has two data points: hit the prescribed sets and reps twice in a row and it will suggest adding load, with an increment sensible for that weight range. It won't guess from a single session.

Where is my data, and is it backed up?

On your iPhone, in the app's own storage. There's no account and no cloud sync — which also means nothing to lose access to. If your iPhone is backed up to iCloud or a computer, the app's data is included in that backup. Deleting the app deletes all of it, permanently.

How do I delete my data?

Delete individual food entries or workout logs inside the app, or delete the app from your iPhone to remove everything at once. See the privacy policy for the small amount of operational data the shared proxy holds and how to have it cleared.

A word on the numbers

Calorie and macronutrient figures are estimates produced by an AI model from a photo or description. Your daily goal comes from a standard population equation (Mifflin-St Jeor), not from measuring your body — treat it as a starting point and correct it against what the scale actually does over a fortnight. None of it is medical, dietary or fitness advice; talk to a qualified professional before making decisions about your diet or training.

Feature requests and bugs

Both are welcome at the address above. Concrete beats general — a photo of the meal that was estimated badly, or the exact steps that produced the wrong screen, is worth more than a description of the feeling.
